NDOL — Privacy Policy

Last updated 2 November 2026

NDOL does not collect your data. Not some of it — none of it. The app has no account, no server and no analytics, and nothing you write is ever sent to the developer. Everything you write stays in NDOL's storage on your own device — the one exception, your own device backup, is described below. The only places NDOL ever reaches out to are Apple's own services: to check whether you have bought the optional Premium unlock and, where the law calls for it, to ask iOS for your broad age range — both described below.

What NDOL stores, and where

All of the following is written to a private storage area on your device that belongs to NDOL, shared only between the app, its widget and its own Control Center capture buttons:

  • The text of your notes, and their titles, tags, folders and topics
  • Voice recordings you make, and the text NDOL transcribes from them
  • Handwriting and drawings in your notebooks
  • Photos, document scans and PDF pages you add to a notebook
  • Reminders, alarms, due dates and daily tasks you create
  • Your settings: accent colour, appearance and capture preferences
  • A small file for the widgets holding only what they draw: a short title for each task, reminder and alarm (for a note, its title or first line), dates, and which days you ticked something off. It never contains the rest of a note's text, your handwriting, photos or recordings
  • Ticks you make on the widget, until NDOL applies them to your tasks: which task, done or not, and when you tapped — and which page of a long list each widget is showing
  • A single on/off setting recording whether Premium is unlocked
  • Up to three recent automatic backups of the above

None of it is transmitted anywhere. There is no copy on any server operated by the developer, because there is no such server.

Dictation happens on your device

When you speak a note, NDOL uses Apple's on-device speech recognition and requires it: the app sets requiresOnDeviceRecognition and will refuse to transcribe, with an explanation, rather than fall back to sending your audio to a server. If your device or language cannot transcribe locally, dictation is unavailable instead of going online.

In-app purchases

NDOL is free to download. It offers one optional in-app purchase — NDOL Premium, a single one-time purchase, not a subscription — which unlocks additional features. You never have to buy it, and nothing you have already written is taken away if you don't.

The purchase is handled entirely by Apple. NDOL uses StoreKit, Apple's own in-app purchase system, which is part of iOS. It is not an advertising, analytics or third-party tracking service. When you buy or restore Premium:

  • Apple takes the payment under your own Apple Account. The developer never receives your card details, your name, your email address or your billing address, and has no way to obtain them.
  • The only thing NDOL records is a single on/off setting on your device saying that Premium is unlocked. That setting never leaves the device.
  • NDOL asks Apple whether your Apple Account owns the purchase. That question goes to Apple, not to the developer, and the answer comes back as yes or no. Apple's handling of it is covered by Apple's privacy policy.
  • Apple gives the developer sales and payment reports. Those are aggregate and financial — units sold, revenue by region — and do not identify individual buyers.

Refunds are handled by Apple, not by the developer, at reportaproblem.apple.com. See the terms of use for what Premium includes.

Permissions NDOL asks for

  • Microphone — to record a voice note. Only while you are recording.
  • Speech recognition — to turn that recording into text, on the device.
  • Camera — only when you scan a page into a notebook.
  • Photos — NDOL uses the system photo picker, so it never asks for access to your library. You choose a picture and only that picture is handed to the app.
  • Notifications — to deliver reminders you set.
  • Alarms (iOS 26 and later) — so alarms you set can ring through Silent Mode. On earlier versions of iOS this permission does not exist and alarms arrive as notifications.

Each is requested at the moment you first use the feature, and NDOL works without any of them if you decline.

NDOL does not use

  • Accounts, sign-in, or any personal details — NDOL never asks who you are
  • Advertising, ad networks, or advertising identifiers
  • Analytics, crash reporting, or usage tracking of any kind. (If you have turned on Share iPhone Analytics with app developers in iOS Settings, Apple may give the developer anonymous crash information about NDOL. That comes from Apple, not from NDOL, and never contains your notes.)
  • Location services
  • Cookies or web tracking. Because NDOL does not track you on its own or across other apps and websites, it has nothing to change in response to a browser's Do Not Track signal
  • Any third-party SDK, library or service. The only frameworks NDOL uses that talk to a network at all are Apple's own, built into iOS: StoreKit, for the in-app purchase described above, and Declared Age Range, for the age signal described below

Because NDOL does not track you, it never asks for App Tracking Transparency permission, and it shares nothing with anyone — there is no third party to share it with.

Keeping, and deleting, your notes

Your notes stay until you delete them. Deleting a note also deletes its voice recording. NDOL keeps up to three recent automatic backups so a bad day is recoverable; older ones are discarded as new ones are made.

One thing NDOL deletes on its own: when you tick off a repeating daily task, that record of having done it is kept for three days and is then permanently deleted, so the list you see each morning is the work still ahead of you. Nothing else is removed for you — your notes, your notebooks and your other tasks stay until you delete them yourself.

You can erase everything at once. Settings has an Erase all data option that removes every note, notebook, page, handwriting stroke, imported image and PDF, voice recording, reminder, task, local backup and setting from your device in one step. It asks you to confirm first, and because there is no cloud copy it cannot be undone — so export anything you want to keep before using it. Erasing your data does not cancel a Premium purchase; that is held by Apple against your Apple Account and can be restored.

Deleting the NDOL app deletes everything it holds. Because there is no cloud copy, that is permanent and cannot be undone by the developer or by anyone else. If you want to keep your notes, export them first — Settings has a full text export, and notebooks export as PDF or as a restorable backup file.

How your notes are protected

Everything NDOL stores sits in its own container on your device, protected by iOS file encryption and your device passcode. NDOL adds no password of its own, and there is no server anywhere holding a copy — so there is nothing of yours to breach except the device in your hand.

iOS offers several levels of file protection and NDOL does not use the same one for everything. Being specific about that matters more than sounding secure:

  • Voice recordings use the strictest level — unreadable while your device is locked.
  • Automatic backups sit one level below, so that a backup already being written can finish even if you lock the device mid-save.
  • Note text, handwriting and page images stay readable once you have unlocked the device at least once since it was switched on. This is the standard protection iOS gives app data, and it is what lets NDOL finish saving a note if you lock the device mid-write.

If your device has no passcode set, iOS file protection does nothing — for NDOL or for any other app.

What the Lock Screen can show

The "Up Next" Lock Screen widget shows the next thing coming up, including its name — for example "Swim practice · 6:00 AM". A Lock Screen is visible to anyone holding your phone, including while it is locked, so this is worth knowing about.

You can turn it off: NDOL → Settings → Lock Screen → Show details on Lock Screen. With it off, the widget shows only the kind and the time, never the name — and the same switch covers ringing alarms and notifications (below). Nothing is sent anywhere either way — the widget reads the same on-device file everything else does.

Notifications show what the note says, unless you turn that off. Reminders, daily tasks and (before iOS 26) alarms arrive as notifications, and by default the notification text is the note's own words — that is what makes it useful. With Show details on Lock Screen off, they say only what they are ("Alarm", "Reminder", "Due today", "Daily task") and never the note's words. NDOL cannot tell whether your phone is locked, so this applies to banners while it is unlocked too. Notifications already delivered before you turn it off keep their words until you clear them. If you would rather keep the words when unlocked and hide them only when locked, leave the switch on and use iOS instead: Settings › Notifications › NDOL › Show Previews, set to When Unlocked or Never.

A ringing alarm shows its name, unless you turn that off. On iOS 26 and later, NDOL's alarms are real system alarms. While one rings, iOS shows the start of the alarm's text full-screen, on the Lock Screen too — the same as a named alarm in any alarm app — and iOS's Show Previews setting does not hide it. With Show details on Lock Screen off, a ringing alarm says only "Alarm" (or "Daily task"). Turning the switch on or off updates alarms you have already set, except one that is ringing or snoozing at that moment, which updates the next time you open NDOL.

Your notes are in iPhone search

NDOL adds your notes to your device's own search index, so that searching from the Home Screen finds a note without you opening NDOL first. That is the point of the feature, but it has a consequence worth stating plainly: a note's title and its text can appear in iPhone search results, and in Siri Suggestions, outside the app.

This still happens entirely on your device — the index belongs to iOS, not to NDOL, and nothing is sent anywhere. But it is not inside NDOL's private container either, and if your device is set to allow search from the Lock Screen, results can be visible there.

You can turn it off at any time: Settings › Siri & Search › NDOL, and switch off Show App in Search and Show Content in Search. Archived notes are never indexed, and deleting a note removes it from the index.

Siri shortcuts. NDOL offers shortcuts such as Speak a Note and Type a Note that you can run with Siri. iOS learns which shortcuts you use and when, the same as for any app. That stays on your device and NDOL is not told about it.

Links, sharing, exporting and copying

Tapping a link — to this policy, the terms or the support page, or a web address inside your own note — opens it in your browser, outside NDOL. NDOL itself does not load the page or send anything with it.

When you share a note, export a notebook as a PDF or text, or save a backup file, you choose where that copy goes — another app, AirDrop, Files, Mail, Messages, or a person. From that moment it is outside NDOL and outside this policy, and it is governed by wherever you sent it. NDOL cannot reach it or take it back.

Tapping Copy puts a note's text on your device's clipboard, which any app you paste into can read. NDOL never reads your clipboard — it only ever writes to it, and only when you tap Copy.

Device backups

One honest exception to "nothing leaves the device": if you use iCloud Backup or back your device up to a computer, NDOL's storage is included, the same as any other app's. That copy is made by Apple under your own Apple Account and is governed by Apple's privacy policy. The developer has no access to it.

Who this app is for

NDOL is intended for users aged 13 and over. It is not directed to children under 13, it is not in the App Store Kids Category, and it does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone — including children — because it does not collect personal information at all.

Age checks required by law

Some US states have laws that ask app stores to confirm a person's age range and pass it on to the apps they download. Where iOS provides it, NDOL may ask iOS — through Apple's own Declared Age Range feature — which broad age range the person using the device falls into (for example under 13, 13 to 15, 16 to 17, or 18 and over) and whether a parent or guardian has approved the download.

  • The answer comes from iOS and Apple, not from the developer. NDOL never sees a birth date, an ID, a name or an email address. It receives only the age range, and only if the person using the device chooses to share it, or the law requires iOS to.
  • NDOL uses the answer on the device, only to follow those laws and its own 13-and-over rule. It is not sent to the developer, not shared with anyone, and not used for anything else — not for advertising, analytics or tracking, none of which NDOL has.
  • Apple's handling of age information is covered by Apple's privacy policy.

If the answer shows that the person using NDOL is younger than the app allows, NDOL may limit what it does for them, or ask them to stop using it, as the law and Apple's rules require.

Your rights

Privacy laws such as the GDPR and the CCPA give you rights to access, correct, export and delete personal data a company holds about you. The developer of NDOL holds no personal data about you, so there is nothing to request. Your notes are already entirely in your possession: you can read, change, export and delete them yourself at any time, in the app. NDOL does not sell or share personal information, because it has none to sell or share.

Changes to this policy

This policy describes what the current version of NDOL actually does. If a future version changes it — cloud sync, an account, or anything at all that sends your notes off your device — this page will be updated before that version is released, and the change will be described here rather than made quietly.
