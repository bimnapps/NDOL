Pricing and Premium

How much does NDOL cost?

NDOL is free to download, and the whole app works without paying. There is one optional in-app purchase, NDOL Premium, at US$4.99. It is a one-time purchase, not a subscription — nothing renews, nothing expires, and there is nothing to cancel. Prices in other countries are set by Apple for your region and may differ; the price shown in the app is always the price you pay. There is no advertising in NDOL.

Nothing you write is ever deleted, or held hostage by whether you have paid. The timeline limit hides: notes older than 30 days stay on your device, stay in search, stay in your exports and backups, and come straight back if you unlock Premium. The other limits are caps on how many you can have going at once — you can't start a fourth notebook or arm a seventh alarm on the free tier, but nothing you already made is taken away, and archiving a finished notebook frees a slot. A repeating daily task that rings counts as a task, not as one of the six alarms.

Free alarms and reminders reach 7 days ahead. On the free tier the calendar for an alarm or a reminder opens today and the next seven days; later days are greyed, and tapping one shows what Premium adds. Repeating alarms, repeating daily tasks, due dates, calendar entries and one-time tasks have no such limit. Anything already set further out — for example while you had Premium — keeps ringing as normal.

A one-time task counts on its own, not against the six. A task you add as "One time" stays on your list until you tick it off — and if you tick it by mistake it stays, crossed out, until midnight so you can untick it. It comes out of its own allowance of three on the free tier, so adding one never uses up room for an ordinary task, and the other way round.

Snooze is free, at every tier, deliberately. A limit that could stop you postponing an alarm that is ringing right now would be a phone that rings until it is paid for, so there isn't one. Both the button and the choice of 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes in Settings are part of the free app.

I already bought Premium — how do I get it on a new device?

Sign in to the same Apple Account, then open NDOL → Settings → Restore Purchases (on iPhone, Settings is in the Profile tab). Premium is tied to your Apple Account, so you never pay twice. Restoring asks Apple for your App Store password; that is normal and it is why the button is there rather than running automatically.

I paid and nothing unlocked.

Try Settings → Restore Purchases first — that fixes almost every case, including a purchase that needed a parent's approval through Ask to Buy, which arrives after you tap Buy rather than straight away. If it still hasn't unlocked, check you're signed in to the Apple Account you bought with, then email bimnapps@icloud.com and say roughly when you bought it.

How do I get a refund?

Refunds for anything bought on the App Store are handled by Apple, not by the developer. Request one at reportaproblem.apple.com. If Apple refunds the purchase, Premium features switch off again — but your notes, notebooks and pages are all still there.

Can my family share Premium?

Not currently — Premium is not set up for Family Sharing, so each Apple Account buys it separately.

How do I cancel?

There is nothing to cancel. Premium is a one-time purchase, so you are never billed again.

Common questions

Where are my notes stored? Can I get them on another device?

Notes are stored on the device that made them. NDOL has no account and no sync, so notes do not appear on a second device on their own. To move them: Settings → Export all notes writes a text file of everything, and a notebook can be exported as a PDF or as a backup file you can open on another device.

Can I see my iPad notebooks on my iPhone?

Yes, to read. Notebooks are written on iPad. To bring one to your iPhone, on the iPad open the notebook's menu → Back up notebook, then AirDrop the file to your iPhone or save it to Files and open it there. NDOL asks whether to add it. On iPhone it appears under Home → Notebooks, where you can read every page and zoom in, but not write. It is a copy: changes you make on the iPad later do not reach the iPhone until you send it again.

How do I make sure I don't lose my notes?

NDOL keeps three recent automatic backups on the device, and you can restore from any of them in Settings → Restore from backup. That protects against a mistake inside the app — it does not protect against losing the device itself. For that, keep iCloud Backup switched on in iOS Settings, and export anything you can't afford to lose.

I deleted the app and my notes are gone.

Deleting the app deletes everything it stored. There is no cloud copy and no way to recover it — not by us either, because we never had it. If you had iCloud Backup on before deleting, restoring the device from that backup may bring it back. A Premium purchase is not lost this way: reinstall and use Settings → Restore Purchases.

Dictation says it isn't available.

NDOL only transcribes on the device and will not send your voice to a server, so it needs the offline language installed. Go to iOS Settings → General → Keyboard → Dictation and make sure Dictation is on and your language is downloaded. On some older devices or less common languages, on-device transcription isn't offered by iOS at all — you can still type notes.

Hiding names on my Lock Screen.

The "Up Next" Lock Screen widget shows what is coming next and its name. A Lock Screen can be read by anyone holding the phone, so if you would rather a reminder's name did not appear there, open NDOL → Settings → Lock Screen and turn off Show details on Lock Screen. The widget then shows only the kind and the time — "Reminder · 3:00 PM" — and never the name. The time is still shown; only names are withheld. The same switch makes ringing alarms say only "Alarm" and notifications say only what they are, on the Lock Screen and in banners alike.

My widget is empty.

Open NDOL once — the app writes the small file the widget reads, so a widget added before the app has run has nothing to show yet. If it stays empty, check what it is set to: long-press it, choose Edit Widget, and switch between Tasks, Reminders and Alarms. Tasks shows what you have to do today — both the tasks you put on a date and your repeating daily list — while Reminders and Alarms show only their own kind, so a widget set to Reminders is empty when nothing is coming up.

Finished daily tasks disappear after a few days. Is that a bug?

No — it is deliberate, and it is the one place NDOL deletes something for you. When you tick a repeating daily task off, it stays on today's list until midnight, then moves to Finished (the clock button on the Tasks screen). It stays there for three days and is then permanently deleted. It is not hidden and it is not recoverable.

That is why a task's "days in a row" figure and its row of dots only go back a few days: there is no older history to draw on. Everything else in NDOL works the other way round — notes, one-time tasks and notebooks are never deleted to enforce anything, and a note you archive or a task you stop repeating keeps all of its contents. Finished daily tasks are the exception, on purpose, so the morning list starts clean.

Ticking tasks on the widget.

Every task on the Tasks widget has a circle you can tap — repeating ones and one-time ones alike. A tick shows straight away. For a one-time task the widget doesn't touch your notes (that is what keeps it fast and inside the small amount of memory a widget is allowed); it records the tick, and NDOL applies it to the task the next time you open the app. If NDOL is already open when you tick, it catches up next time you come back to it. Tapping the words instead of the circle opens the task.

Long lists. Widgets can't scroll, so on the medium and large sizes a list longer than the widget shows arrows on the right: tap down for the next items. It goes back to the first page each new day.

The Lock Screen / Control Center button.

NDOL adds two controls: one for speaking a note and one for typing. To add one to the Lock Screen, long-press the Lock Screen → Customise → tap the ⊖ on a bottom-corner button → tap + → search NDOL. For Control Center, swipe down from the top-right, long-press an empty area → Add a Control → search NDOL.

My alarm didn't go off.

Alarms and reminders are delivered by iOS, not by NDOL, and iOS can delay or suppress them — a device that was off, out of battery, in Low Power Mode, in a Focus, or with notification permission turned off. On iOS 26 and later NDOL uses real system alarms, which ring in Silent Mode and, once set, keep ringing on schedule even if you never open NDOL again — except that iOS will not ring alarms for an app that is hidden or set to require Face ID or a passcode. On iOS 18–25 alarms are notifications, which follow your ringer switch and Focus settings — keep the ringer on. Wherever alarms arrive as notifications (iOS 18–25, or iOS 26 with alarm permission off), iOS holds a limited number per app: there is room for about 32 repeating alarm and daily-task times at once (an everyday alarm is one, a weekday alarm one per day). Any beyond that are kept, and NDOL lines up their next few rings soonest first, topping them up each time you open it or iOS gives it background time; a ring that has not been lined up yet does not go off, so keep Background App Refresh on for NDOL. Repeating alarms take those permanent places before task reminders do. On iOS 26 with alarm permission on, every alarm is a full-screen system alarm (except ones set to no sound) unless iOS itself declines to hold more, in which case any it declines arrive as notifications. Don't rely on NDOL as your only alarm for anything that matters. See the terms of use.

Is any of this sent anywhere?

No. Nothing you write is ever sent to us — no account, no server, no analytics, no ads. The only things NDOL ever asks anyone for are Apple's own services: whether your Apple Account owns the Premium unlock, and, where the law calls for it, your broad age range (a bracket such as 13 to 15 — never a birth date). The one thing that does copy your notes off the device is your own iCloud or computer backup, made by Apple under your Apple Account, which we have no access to. See the privacy policy.

Why is NDOL asking about my age, and is it for kids?

NDOL is for people aged 13 and over. Some US states now require app stores to check a person's age range and pass it to apps, and where iOS provides it, NDOL may ask iOS for that range — a bracket such as 13 to 15 or 18 and over, never a birth date or an ID. The answer comes from Apple, stays on your device, and is never sent to us. If you are a parent: where the law requires approval for people under 18, Apple asks for it through your Apple Account and Family Sharing. See the privacy policy.
